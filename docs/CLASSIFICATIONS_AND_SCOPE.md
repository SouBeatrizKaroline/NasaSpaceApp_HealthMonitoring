# Classifications, Professional Scope, and Permissions

## Design principle

Use original, plain-language questions and preserve clinician judgment. A classification system is not a substitute for an examination, scope of practice, or mission-approved procedure. This prototype does not embed proprietary taxonomies, codes, diagnostic criteria, or treatment tables.

| Resource | Potential use in a future reviewed design | Prototype boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NANDA International | Nursing assessment terminology and care-plan interoperability | No labels, codes, definitions, translations, or copied taxonomy; obtain a license and authorized review |
| ICD-11 | Clinician-authored condition coding and interoperable records | No autonomous coding; confirm licensing, implementation, and authorized translation requirements |
| ICF | Describe body functions, activities, participation, and environmental context | Use original functional prompts; do not reproduce or adapt protected classification content without permission |
| CBDF | Brazilian physiotherapy terminology reference | Brazilian context only; no codes or claim that it is a mission standard |
| ICNP / ICNP integration resources | Nursing documentation and terminology interoperability | No embedded terminology set; confirm access and licensing |
| DSM-5-TR | Clinician reference in behavioral-health practice | No copied criteria, diagnostic scoring, or diagnosis from self-report |
| AOTA Occupational Therapy Practice Framework | Describe occupations, performance patterns, and context | Functional prompts only; clinician owns any professional assessment |
| Manchester and ESI | Emergency-care context on Earth | Not used as spacecraft triage protocols |
| ATC, Beers, NCPT, GLIM, TNM, BI-RADS, LI-RADS, PI-RADS, GOLD, GINA, and specialist criteria | Possible future specialist integrations after validation and permissions | Outside the prototype; no medication selection or specialist interpretation |

## Rights and licensing

NANDA-I requires permission for use of its taxonomy in software. WHO copyright and translation permissions vary by classification and use; consult the current WHO licensing terms before embedding or adapting content. The ICF classification is not a physiotherapy diagnosis. CBDF is jurisdiction-specific and should be reviewed against current COFFITO publications. APA material is copyrighted. Check current license terms for all external terminology and standards before distribution.

## Clinical governance

Any future implementation should identify a clinical owner, validate each question with the relevant profession, document intended population and environment, manage language review, and maintain traceable versions. The software must clearly distinguish user-reported observations from clinician findings and coded diagnoses.
