# Gravity and Mission Operations

## Mission context matters

Gravity exposure, mission phase, and time since transition can change the interpretation of symptoms and functional performance. Record context explicitly; never treat gravity level as a diagnosis.

| Context | Health and task considerations to monitor | Appropriate record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Microgravity / free fall | Fluid redistribution, spatial orientation, balance, sensorimotor adaptation, exercise loading, mobility and restraint needs | Symptoms, functional impact, phase, timing, personal baseline, selected measurements |
| Transition to lunar gravity | Adaptation to partial gravity, gait and balance, task execution, orthostatic symptoms, operational readiness | Transition time, task effects, symptom onset and recovery |
| Surface operations | EVA workload, suit constraints, dust and environmental exposure, hydration and fatigue context | Activity, exposure, symptoms, work/rest context |
| Return to higher gravity | Readaptation, balance, mobility, cardiovascular tolerance, safe completion of mission tasks | Functional observations and clinician-configured follow-up |

NASA describes gravity fields among the five hazards of human spaceflight. Bone, muscle, cardiovascular, sensorimotor, and ocular outcomes are influenced by interacting mission factors. The interface therefore asks what changed and what task is affected rather than asserting a universal gravity-based threshold.

## Five hazard context

- **Radiation:** log mission exposure context only when an authorized measurement feed exists; do not infer individual future disease.
- **Isolation and confinement:** support private, voluntary mood, sleep, stress, workload, and team-support check-ins.
- **Distance from Earth:** retain local records, clear instructions, and procedures approved for the mission's communications plan.
- **Gravity fields:** capture transition, adaptation, and functional effects.
- **Hostile or closed environments:** allow mission-configured context such as cabin environment or exposure reports, with provenance.

Hazards interact; a record should preserve multiple relevant factors without claiming causation.

## Procedure configuration

Operational response thresholds, sensor integrations, access roles, routing, offline synchronization, and contingency behavior are mission-specific and must be set by the responsible clinical and engineering authorities. Do not use demo labels as flight procedures.
