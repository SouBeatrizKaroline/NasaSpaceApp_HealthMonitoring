# Challenge Evidence and Source Register

Reviewed for the prototype concept on 2026-11-15. Sources below support design context; they do not establish that this prototype is clinically validated or approved for flight.

## Challenge requirements

The [2026 Health Monitoring Software challenge](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/health-monitoring-software/?tab=details) calls for a visual tool that helps crew assess health, collect relevant indicators, and support actions during missions. The challenge highlights self-assessment, clear presentation, understandable instructions, and health indicators such as blood pressure, heart rate, blood glucose, temperature, sleep, and mood. See also the official [challenge resources tab](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/health-monitoring-software/?tab=resources) and [challenge overview video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RERG3gEmdC8).

## Primary mission-health references

- NASA, [Five Hazards of Human Spaceflight](https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazards/): radiation, isolation and confinement, distance from Earth, gravity fields, and hostile or closed environments.
- NASA, [Human System Risks](https://www.nasa.gov/hhp/human-system-risks/): evidence and risk summaries across physiology, behavioral performance, and radiation.
- NASA, [Human Spaceflight and Aviation Standards](https://www.nasa.gov/ochmo/human-spaceflight-and-aviation-standards/): NASA-STD-3001 standards. Applicability depends on mission and system context; this prototype makes no compliance claim.
- NASA, [Aerospace Medical Certification Standard](https://www.nasa.gov/organizations/ochmo/aerospace-medical-certification-standard/): selection, certification, and recertification context; it is not a software checklist.
- NASA, [Integrated Medical Model](https://www.nasa.gov/general/nasas-integrated-medical-model-imm/): mission-level medical risk and resource planning.
- NASA NTRS, [IMPACT 1.0 Medical Condition List](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20220015765): an exploration planning resource for medical-condition risk and capability trade-offs, not a patient-facing diagnostic catalog.

## Evidence qualifications reflected in the design

- NASA describes a limited set of in-situ laboratory demonstrations; these do not mean that a complete routine laboratory service is available in orbit. See [in-situ lab analysis](https://www.nasa.gov/glenn/glenn-expertise-space-exploration/human-health-performance/exploration-medical-technologies/in-situ-lab-analysis/) and [rHEALTH One demonstration](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/glenn/nasa-manages-astronaut-health-with-effective-diagnostics-research/).
- NASA's portable sequencing demonstrations examined microbial samples; they are not routine individual genome or radiation-mutation screening. See [DNA in space](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/iss-research/station-science-101-studying-dna-in-space/).
- Astronaut-performed ultrasound and remote guidance have been studied; ultrasound is not represented here as a universal diagnostic standard. See [ultrasound in space](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/ultrasound-scans-in-space-transform-medicine-on-earth/).
- SANS involves observed ocular and fluid-shift changes with mechanisms and long-term outcomes still under study. A reported prevalence of signs is not the same as a rate of confirmed clinical diagnoses. See NASA's [SANS overview](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/risk-of-spaceflight-associated-neuro-ocular-syndrome-sans/).
- Space adaptation sickness may occur in early flight; NASA materials describe a broad range of timing and symptoms. The prototype does not recommend medication. See [NASA technical brief](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/ochmo-mtb-004-space-adaptation-sickness-sas.pdf).
- A jugular venous thrombosis was reported in an ISS research context and managed with flight and ground medical collaboration. This case is not a basis for self-diagnosis or medication advice. See the [clinical report](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/nejmc1905875).
- Medication stability depends on product, packaging, and storage; this project makes no fixed spaceflight degradation claim. See [published review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26546565/) and [later review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39043673/).
- Deep-space communication delay can limit real-time consultation. Planning must account for mission-specific delay and autonomy. See [NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20220013418).

## External standards and professional resources

- WHO [ICF](https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/international-classification-of-functioning-disability-and-health) supports a function-centered view of body functions, activities, participation, and environmental context.
- NANDA International [use and licensing](https://nanda.org/use-and-licensing/) explains licensing requirements for its taxonomy.
- COFFITO [CBDF FAQ](https://cbdf.coffito.gov.br/faq) and [Resolution 610/2025](https://www.coffito.gov.br/nsite/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/resolucao-610.pdf) concern a Brazilian physiotherapy classification.
- ICN [ICNP resources](https://www.icn.ch/how-we-do-it/projects/ehealth-icnp) describe nursing terminology and its integration pathway.
- AOTA [Occupational Therapy Practice Framework](https://www.aota.org/practice/domain-and-process) provides occupation and participation framing.
- IBM Quantum [QAOA tutorial](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/tutorials/quantum-approximate-optimization-algorithm) and [Qiskit Optimization tutorials](https://qiskit-community.github.io/qiskit-optimization/tutorials/index.html) inform the optional synthetic scheduling experiment.
- NASA's [brand center](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/) and [media guidance](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/) inform the independent-project presentation.

## Source maintenance
